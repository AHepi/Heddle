## rewrite

Rewrite the draft (read_draft) into classic style and write the new
version (write_draft) as a NEW note — never overwrite the original;
the before/after pair is the evidence the pass happened.

The rewrite is a re-seeing, not a synonym pass. For each finding from
critique, in order:

1. Decide what concrete thing each passage is showing, and show it.
   Cut the roadmap; open with the thing itself or with a question the
   passage answers. Write to an intelligent equal — no apologizing, no
   covering your anatomy.
2. Unpack every chunk critique flagged: spell out the abbreviation,
   gloss the term in a few words, supply the missing step. If a step
   cannot be supplied without inventing content, mark it "[gap: what
   is missing]" rather than filling it with a guess.
3. Wake the zombie nouns: give each embalmed verb back its verb and
   its doer ("effect an affirmation" → *affirm*). Prefer the active —
   but keep a deliberate passive where it preserves given-before-new
   or drops an irrelevant doer; the passive is a tool, not a crime.
4. Rebuild flagged sentences for working memory: split
   center-embeddings, prefer right-branching order, keep parallel
   structure parallel, and put the emphatic word at the END of the
   sentence. End strong.
5. Restore the arcs: state the topic early, call the same thing by
   the same name, and make every inter-sentence relation explicit
   with the right connective (*so, but, because, then*).
6. Apply the usage rulings exactly as critique reported them: break
   the folklore rules freely, hold the line on the defended
   distinctions.

The rewrite must say everything the draft said and nothing it didn't
— meaning is the author's; only the window is yours. Note in your
summary any place you had to choose between readings, labeled as an
overridable assumption.

Outcome: rewritten.
