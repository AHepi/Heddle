## critique

Read the draft (read_draft) and hold it against Pinker's *Sense of
Style*. You diagnose here; you fix nothing — rewrite is the next step's
job. Every finding quotes the draft's own words as evidence: a finding
without a quoted line is a taste report, not a critique.

Work the checklist in order, biggest gears first:

1. **Classic style.** Is the writer showing the reader something
   concrete, as an equal? Flag prose that talks about the writing
   instead of the world: metadiscourse ("In this essay, I will…"),
   signposting that says what will be said and then what was said,
   and professional narcissism (the field's internal debates standing
   in for the subject).
2. **The curse of knowledge.** Flag every term, abbreviation, or chunk
   a non-specialist reader cannot unpack: jargon without a gloss,
   missing steps that seem too obvious to mention, scenes the writer
   sees clearly and never shows.
3. **Zombie nouns and hedges.** Flag nominalizations embalming spry
   verbs ("effect an affirmation" for *affirm*), morbidly obese phrases
   ("in the event that" for *if*), wads of hedging fluff (*somewhat,
   rather, presumably, I would argue*), and soggy intensifiers (*very*).
4. **The tree.** Flag sentences that tax working memory:
   center-embedded clauses, left-branching piles that make the reader
   hold open brackets, and garden paths. Order should run
   given-before-new and light-before-heavy; the heavy constituent
   belongs at the end.
5. **Arcs of coherence.** Flag paragraphs where the topic shifts
   without warning, where the same thing wears different names, or
   where two sentences sit side by side with no connective making
   their relation explicit. When in doubt, more connection, not less.
6. **Usage.** Do NOT flag split infinitives, terminal prepositions,
   sentences opening with a conjunction, or singular *they* — those
   rules are folklore. DO flag misused words that make readers
   conclude the worst: *disinterested* for uninterested, *enormity*
   for enormousness, *fortuitous* for fortunate, *refute* for dispute,
   *literally* for figuratively, and their kin.

Report `sound` only if the checklist comes back empty. Otherwise
`needs_work`, with findings ordered by the checklist — an early class
left unfixed makes later polish pointless.

Outcome: sound or needs_work.
